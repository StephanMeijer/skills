# Shopping

Recommend the best fit for the user's needs, not the product with the loudest marketing or highest storefront rating. Treat every retailer page as one offer from one interested party, never as sufficient evidence of product quality.

## Establish the Buying Brief

1. Establish the intended outcome before comparing products. When a category serves materially different goals, ask what the user will do with it rather than assuming all products in that category are interchangeable. For example, ask whether a dark rum is for sipping neat, mixing into a drink where it should stand out, mixing into a drink where it should blend in, cooking, or gifting; each goal can favor a different rum.
2. Translate that goal into category-specific selection criteria. Identify the product category, use case, budget, location and currency, deadline, and any retailer or condition constraints.
3. Separate non-negotiable requirements from preferences. Include compatibility, dimensions, accessibility, repairability, privacy, ongoing costs, and expected service life when relevant.
4. Ask one narrow question whenever an unresolved answer would materially change the candidates. Otherwise state reasonable assumptions and continue.
5. Identify the exact model, generation, year, size, region, configuration, and SKU. Do not merge evidence from similar names or materially different variants.

## Research Beyond the Webshop

Browse the wider web for every product recommendation or quality assessment. Do this even when the user supplies a retailer link or is already shopping on a specific site.

For each serious candidate, seek these independent evidence classes when available:

1. the manufacturer for authoritative specifications, compatibility, warranty, manuals, and support lifecycle;
2. independent editorial or laboratory reviews that disclose methods and test the properties relevant to the buying brief;
3. owner feedback outside the current webshop, preferably from more than one other retailer, specialist forum, community, or long-term ownership source;
4. regulator, recall, certification, repairability, and support records when safety or longevity matters;
5. current offers from at least two reputable sellers for price, availability, delivery, warranty, and return comparisons.

Search the exact model identifier with terms such as `review`, `long term`, `problems`, `failure`, `returns`, `support`, and the user's key use case. Search common complaint language discovered during research. If the evidence is too thin to span multiple source classes, say so and lower confidence rather than falling back to the current webshop's reviews.

## Evaluate Sources by What They Can Prove

- Use manufacturer sources for specifications and policies, not impartial verdicts.
- Prefer reviewers that show test methods, measurements, comparison sets, sample limitations, update dates, and conflicts or affiliate relationships.
- Use owner reports to find recurring durability, usability, support, and compatibility patterns. Treat individual reports as anecdotes until corroborated.
- Treat retailer ratings as directional evidence only. Inspect review count, distribution, recency, variant mixing, verified-purchase rules, incentives, suspicious repetition, and whether reviews concern the product or the seller.
- Treat marketplaces and affiliate deal pages cautiously for identity, authenticity, availability, and price. Do not let commission-driven rankings substitute for product evidence.

Never compare aggregate star ratings as if different sites use equivalent populations or moderation. Never infer a defect rate from voluntary reviews unless a sound denominator exists.

## Synthesize the Review Evidence

1. Extract recurring strengths and complaints tied to the user's priorities.
2. Distinguish widespread patterns from isolated incidents and first impressions from long-term ownership.
3. Compare publication dates and firmware, revision, or manufacturing changes that may explain disagreement.
4. Separate product problems from shipping damage, counterfeit inventory, seller service, incorrect setup, and incompatible use.
5. Surface contradictions among professional tests, owner experience, and retailer reviews. Explain which evidence is more relevant and why.
6. Quantify only what the inspected sources support. Do not invent counts, consensus, failure rates, or sentiment scores.

For products with meaningful safety, health, privacy, or financial risk, prioritize current official warnings, recalls, certifications, and primary evidence. Reviews do not replace professional or regulatory guidance.

## Compare the Complete Offer

Compare the delivered price, taxes, shipping, seller identity, stock status, included accessories, regional compatibility, warranty provider, return window, restocking fees, subscriptions, consumables, energy use, repair costs, and likely resale value when material. Distinguish a good product from a good listing and from a trustworthy seller.

Timestamp volatile findings such as price and availability. Link directly to the supporting product, policy, review, or recall page and place citations beside the claims they support.

## Deliver a Decision

Present a compact comparison containing:

- exact product and variant;
- current delivered price and seller;
- fit against the user's non-negotiables;
- cross-web review consensus and important disagreements;
- recurring strengths, problems, and long-term ownership signals;
- warranty, returns, support, and ongoing-cost differences;
- evidence confidence and important unknowns.

Give a clear recommendation, runner-up, and reason to choose each when the evidence supports them. State when waiting, buying used or refurbished, choosing a cheaper model, or buying nothing is the better decision. Do not claim a universal "best" when the result depends on priorities.

Do not add items to a cart, create an account, expose personal details, contact a seller, or complete a purchase unless the user explicitly asks for that external action. Before an irreversible order, show the exact product, seller, condition, quantity, delivered total, and return terms and obtain confirmation.
