# Shopping

Recommend the best fit for the user's needs, not the loudest marketing or the highest storefront rating. A retailer page is one offer from one interested party, never sufficient evidence of product quality.

## Establish the Buying Brief

1. Before comparing products, establish the intended outcome. When a category serves materially different goals, ask what the user will do with it: a dark rum for sipping neat, standing out in a drink, blending into a drink, cooking, or gifting can each favor a different rum.
2. Turn that goal into category-specific selection criteria: product category, use case, budget, location and currency, deadline, and any retailer or condition constraints.
3. Separate non-negotiable requirements from preferences, including compatibility, dimensions, accessibility, repairability, privacy, ongoing costs, and expected service life when relevant.
4. Ask one narrow question when an unresolved answer would materially change the candidates; otherwise state assumptions and continue.
5. Identify the exact model, generation, year, size, region, configuration, and SKU. Do not merge evidence from similar names or materially different variants.

## Resolve Exact Identifiers First

When the user supplies an EAN, GTIN, UPC, ISBN, SKU, manufacturer part number, model number, or similarly specific identifier, that exact product is the primary research subject.

1. Search the identifier verbatim, including punctuation, and determine whether it is a global identifier, manufacturer identifier, or retailer-specific SKU.
2. Verify it against the manufacturer or another authoritative catalog and establish the exact product name, variant, dimensions, region, generation, and package contents. Do not accept a search engine's substituted or near-match result.
3. Research that exact product's specifications, offers, reviews, known problems, and suitability before any alternative: `De Buyer 5680.28` is that specific 28 cm model, not cookware generally or the whole Mineral B range.
4. Label model-family or neighboring-size evidence as indirect; attribute it to the exact product only when the source explicitly covers that identifier or confirmed variant.
5. Broaden to similar products only after presenting the exact product assessment, when the identifier cannot be resolved, or when the user asks for comparisons or alternatives, and say why.

## Research Beyond the Webshop

Browse the wider web for every recommendation or quality assessment, even when the user supplies a retailer link or is already on a specific site. Use the web search and page-fetch tools you have; without any, say so, give no prices, availability, or review counts, and stop at the buying brief.

For each serious candidate, seek these independent evidence classes when available:

1. the manufacturer for specifications, compatibility, warranty, manuals, and support lifecycle;
2. independent editorial or laboratory reviews that disclose methods and test the properties in the buying brief;
3. owner feedback outside the current webshop, preferably from more than one other retailer, specialist forum, community, or long-term ownership source;
4. regulator, recall, certification, repairability, and support records when safety or longevity matters;
5. current offers from at least two reputable sellers for price, availability, delivery, warranty, and returns.

Search the exact model identifier with `review`, `long term`, `problems`, `failure`, `returns`, `support`, the user's key use case, and complaint language found during research. If the evidence does not span multiple source classes, say so and lower confidence rather than falling back to the current webshop's reviews.

## Evaluate Sources by What They Can Prove

- Manufacturer sources prove specifications and policies, not impartial verdicts.
- Prefer reviewers that show test methods, measurements, comparison sets, sample limitations, update dates, and conflicts or affiliate relationships.
- Use owner reports for recurring durability, usability, support, and compatibility patterns; an individual report is an anecdote until corroborated.
- Retailer ratings are directional only. Inspect review count, distribution, recency, variant mixing, verified-purchase rules, incentives, suspicious repetition, and whether reviews concern the product or the seller.
- Treat marketplaces and affiliate deal pages cautiously for identity, authenticity, availability, and price; commission-driven rankings are not product evidence.

Never compare aggregate star ratings across sites as if they used equivalent populations or moderation. Never infer a defect rate from voluntary reviews without a sound denominator.

## Synthesize the Review Evidence

1. Extract recurring strengths and complaints tied to the user's priorities.
2. Distinguish widespread patterns from isolated incidents, and first impressions from long-term ownership.
3. Compare publication dates and firmware, revision, or manufacturing changes that may explain disagreement.
4. Separate product problems from shipping damage, counterfeit inventory, seller service, incorrect setup, and incompatible use.
5. Surface contradictions among professional tests, owner experience, and retailer reviews, and say which evidence is more relevant and why.
6. Quantify only what the inspected sources support. Do not invent counts, consensus, failure rates, or sentiment scores.

For products with meaningful safety, health, privacy, or financial risk, prioritize current official warnings, recalls, certifications, and primary evidence; reviews do not replace professional or regulatory guidance.

## Compare the Complete Offer

Compare the delivered price, taxes, shipping, seller identity, stock status, included accessories, regional compatibility, warranty provider, return window, restocking fees, subscriptions, consumables, energy use, repair costs, and likely resale value when material. Distinguish a good product, a good listing, and a trustworthy seller.

Timestamp volatile findings such as price and availability. Link directly to the supporting product, policy, review, or recall page, beside the claim it supports.

## Deliver a Decision

Present a compact comparison containing:

- exact product and variant;
- current delivered price and seller;
- fit against the user's non-negotiables;
- cross-web review consensus and important disagreements;
- recurring strengths, problems, and long-term ownership signals;
- warranty, returns, support, and ongoing-cost differences;
- evidence confidence and important unknowns.

Give a recommendation, a runner-up, and the reason to choose each when the evidence supports them. State when waiting, buying used or refurbished, choosing a cheaper model, or buying nothing is the better decision. Do not claim a universal "best" when the result depends on priorities.

Do not add items to a cart, create an account, expose personal details, contact a seller, or complete a purchase unless the user explicitly asks. Before an irreversible order, show the exact product, seller, condition, quantity, delivered total, and return terms and obtain confirmation.
